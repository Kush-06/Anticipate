# Lesson 20: Pension Consolidation

The average UK worker changes jobs around **11 times** during their career. Thanks to Auto-Enrolment, this means you will likely accumulate several different workplace pension pots. Leaving them scattered is a recipe for lost money and high fees.

---

## Why Combine Your Pensions?

| Benefit | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Easier tracking** | One login, one balance to check |
| **Lower fees** | Multiple providers mean multiple admin fees. Moving to a modern provider can save thousands over decades |
| **Better investment choices** | Older pensions often default to cautious funds. A new provider lets you choose funds that match your actual risk tolerance |

---

## The Warning: When NOT to Consolidate

Before you move any money, check if your old pensions have **safeguarded benefits**.

- **Defined Benefit (Final Salary) Pensions:** Never transfer these without independent financial advice. They guarantee you a set income for life
- **Guaranteed Annuity Rates (GARs):** Some older pensions promise a very high payout rate at retirement. Transferring out means losing this guarantee
- **Exit Fees:** Check if your old provider will charge a penalty for leaving

> Use the free, government-backed service **MoneyHelper** if you are unsure about the terms of your old pension.

---

## How to Find and Combine Old Pensions

**Step 1 — Track them down:** If you've lost the paperwork, use the government's official **Pension Tracing Service** on Gov.uk. You just need the name of your old employer.

**Step 2 — Get the details:** Contact your old providers and ask for your current balance, transfer value, and confirmation of any exit fees or lost guarantees.

**Step 3 — Initiate the transfer:** Choose your preferred main pension (current workplace pension or a private SIPP). Ask your new provider to initiate the transfer — they handle the heavy lifting.
