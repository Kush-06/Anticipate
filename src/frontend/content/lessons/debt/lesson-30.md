# Lesson 30: Payoff Strategies — Avalanche vs. Snowball

If you find yourself juggling multiple bad debts, a structured plan is essential. Without one, people tend to make erratic payments across accounts without making real progress.

---

## Financial Triage First

Before applying any strategy:

1. **Stop adding to the debt.** Stop using your credit cards; some people cut them up to prevent use
2. Complete a strict monthly budget to find exactly how much spare cash you have after essential bills and food
3. List every single debt: total balance, minimum monthly payment, and APR

Once you have your spare cash and debt list, choose a strategy. **Both strategies require you to set up direct debits to pay the minimum on every debt.** Your strategy only dictates where your extra money goes.

---

## Strategy 1: The Avalanche Method (The Mathematical Winner)

Focus entirely on the **cost of borrowing**. Throw all spare cash at the debt with the highest APR, regardless of its total balance.

**How it works:**
1. Pay the minimum on everything
2. Apply every extra pound to the highest-APR debt
3. Once cleared, avalanche that money onto the next highest-APR debt

**Why it is recommended:** This is the strictly logical, mathematically optimal approach. By killing the most expensive debt first, you pay less total interest and become debt-free months or years earlier.

---

## Strategy 2: The Snowball Method (The Psychological Winner)

Completely ignore interest rates. Focus on the **number of debts** you owe. Throw all spare cash at the debt with the smallest total balance.

**How it works:**
1. Pay the minimum on everything
2. Apply every extra pound to the smallest balance
3. Once cleared, snowball that money onto the next smallest balance

**Why it is recommended:** Debt is exhausting. The Avalanche Method can feel like a gruelling, endless slog. The Snowball Method creates quick, early victories — clearing a £200 store card in a month gives you a dopamine hit and the motivation to tackle larger balances.

---

## Supercharging Your Payoff: The 0% Balance Transfer

Regardless of which method you choose, your goal is to stop paying interest so 100% of your payments go toward the actual debt.

A **0% Balance Transfer Credit Card** lets you move expensive debt onto a new card with a 0% interest period (often 12 to 28 months).

**Crucial rules:**

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Transfer Fee** | Usually 2% to 4% of the balance — almost always cheaper than paying 25% APR for a year |
| **Never miss a payment** | Missing a single minimum payment immediately strips your 0% rate and bumps you to the standard punitive rate |
| **Know your deadline** | Calculate how much you need to pay monthly to clear the debt before the 0% period ends |
