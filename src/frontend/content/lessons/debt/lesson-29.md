# Lesson 29: The Debt Spectrum — Good, Bad, and the Grey Area

The most damaging myth in personal finance is that all debt is inherently evil and must be avoided at all costs. The crucial difference lies in distinguishing between debt that acts as an **investment** and debt that acts as an **anchor**.

---

## What is "Good" Debt?

Good debt is an investment in your financial future. It provides long-term value, typically carries lower interest rates, and is tied to an asset that either generates income or appreciates over time.

**Mortgages**
A mortgage is the classic example of good debt. Over the long term, property in the UK generally appreciates. By putting down a deposit and borrowing the rest, you secure an asset you could never afford in cash. While you pay interest to the bank, the historic increase in property value often outpaces the cost of borrowing — and you are building equity rather than paying a landlord's mortgage through rent.

**UK Student Loans**
UK student loans should not be viewed as traditional debt — they are more accurately described as a "graduate contribution" or a "graduate tax."
- Does not appear on your credit file
- Will not stop you from getting a mortgage
- You only repay a percentage of income **above a specific threshold**
- If you lose your job, repayments immediately drop to zero
- After 30 to 40 years, the remaining balance is **entirely wiped**

---

## The Grey Area: Car Finance (PCP & HP)

Cars are depreciating assets — they lose value the moment you drive off the forecourt.

- **PCP:** You pay to cover depreciation over a set term without building ownership. You pay continuous interest without gaining a permanent asset
- **HP:** You pay fixed instalments and own the car outright at the end

Whether car finance is "good" or "bad" depends entirely on the APR and necessity. A low-APR deal for a car essential to your livelihood is justifiable. A 14% APR PCP deal purely for a luxury badge leans heavily into bad debt.

---

## What is "Bad" Debt?

Bad debt drains your wealth. It is used to purchase depreciating consumer goods or fund everyday living, weaponised by punitive, compounding interest rates.

**Payday Loans**
The most toxic financial products on the market. While the FCA introduced a price cap in 2015, they remain extremely dangerous. Having a payday loan on your credit file makes lenders highly reluctant to offer you a mortgage.

**Credit Cards (When Carried Over)**
Credit cards are excellent tools for Section 75 consumer protection and building credit history. They become bad debt the moment you fail to clear the balance in full every month. A standard UK credit card charges around **25% APR**.

**The Minimum Payment Trap**
Credit card companies intentionally set minimum payments very low (often 1% to 2.5% of the balance plus interest). With a £3,000 balance at 22% APR paying only the minimum, it will take over **20 years** to clear and you will pay more than **£4,000 in interest alone**.

**Unarranged Overdrafts**
Following FCA rule changes in 2020, almost all major UK high street banks now charge approximately **39.9% APR** on overdrafts — more expensive than most credit cards.

---

## The Golden Rule

> If the interest rate you are paying is significantly higher than the financial return the item will bring you over time, it is bad debt and should be cleared as an absolute priority.
