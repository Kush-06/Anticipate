# Lesson 31: Getting Free Help — What to Do When You Are Overwhelmed

There is a distinct line between "managing debt" and being in a "debt crisis."

If your income does not cover your essential living costs (rent, council tax, food, heating) **and** the minimum payments on your debts, payoff strategies like the Avalanche or Snowball methods will not work. You cannot mathematically budget your way out of a deficit.

---

## The Golden Rule: Avoid Fee-Charging Debt Management Companies

When you search for "debt help" online, the top results are often paid advertisements from commercial companies that charge hefty upfront fees or take a large percentage of your monthly payments as "admin costs." Every pound you pay them is a pound not going toward your actual debt.

> **You should never, ever pay a commercial company for debt advice.**

There are fully funded charities equipped to negotiate with your creditors, stop harassment, and help you find a legal route out of debt. They are funded by a levy on the financial services industry — the banks pay for them so you don't have to.

---

## Who to Contact

| Organisation | Best For |
|---|---|
| **StepChange Debt Charity** | UK's largest debt charity. Comprehensive online "Debt Remedy" tool for a full assessment without needing to call. Also offers full phone support. |
| **Citizens Advice** | Best if your debt is complicated by other issues (illegal eviction, benefits disputes, employment tribunals). Face-to-face support in local branches across the country. |
| **National Debtline** | Telephone-based service focused on empowering you to handle debts yourself, with legal frameworks, template letters, and budgeting advice. |

---

## What Actually Happens When You Get Help

**1. Triage and Immediate Protection**
If you are facing an imminent emergency (bailiffs, eviction notice, frozen bank account), the advisor deals with the immediate threat first. They can inform your creditors you are seeking advice, which usually pauses collection activity.

**2. The Standard Financial Statement (SFS)**
The advisor walks you through a detailed budget. This is not to judge your spending — it uses an industry-standard framework to prove to your creditors exactly how much you can actually afford to pay after keeping a roof over your head and food on the table.

**3. Recommendation of Solutions**
Based on your SFS, the advisor presents your legal options with pros, cons, and credit file impacts. The final decision is always yours. They will never force you into a solution.

**4. Implementation**
If you choose a managed solution, the charity will often set it up for you — writing to creditors, asking them to freeze interest and charges, and managing negotiations on your behalf.

---

## The Tools at Their Disposal

**Breathing Space (The Debt Respite Scheme)**
A government scheme introduced in 2021. If you pass checks with a debt advisor, you receive **60 days of legal protection** — creditors cannot add interest or fees, and are legally banned from contacting you or taking enforcement action. There is also a specialised Mental Health Crisis Breathing Space that lasts as long as you are receiving crisis treatment, plus 30 days.

**Debt Management Plan (DMP)**
An informal agreement where you make one affordable monthly payment to the charity (e.g., £50/month) and they divide it fairly among creditors. The charity asks creditors to freeze interest, meaning every penny pays down the actual balance.

**Debt Relief Order (DRO)**
A formal insolvency solution for people with low incomes, few assets (like no home ownership), and debts under £50,000. Debts are paused for 12 months; if your situation hasn't improved, the debts are entirely written off. As of April 2024, the £90 application fee was **abolished** — it is now entirely free to access.
