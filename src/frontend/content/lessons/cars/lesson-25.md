# Lesson 25: The True Cost of Driving

The purchase price of a car is just the entry ticket. Keeping a car legally and safely on UK roads requires a constant stream of cash across four key areas.

---

## 1. The MOT (Ministry of Transport Test)

Every car over **three years old** must pass an annual MOT test — a strict safety and environmental inspection.

- **Government-capped fee:** £54.85 maximum for the inspection itself
- **The reality:** That fee only pays for the mechanic's time to look at the car. If brake pads are worn, tyres are bald, or emissions are too high, the car fails. You then pay separately for all repairs before it can return to the road. A "cheap" MOT can easily become a **£400 repair bill**
- **The law:** Driving without a valid MOT is illegal, carrying a fine of up to £1,000 and **immediately invalidating your car insurance**

---

## 2. Vehicle Excise Duty (VED / Road Tax)

You must tax your vehicle to drive or even park it on a public road.

| Vehicle Type | Tax Rule |
|---|---|
| New petrol/diesel (Year 1) | Based on CO2 emissions — a highly polluting car can exceed £5,000 |
| New petrol/diesel (Year 2+) | Standard flat rate of **£200/year** (2026/27 tax year) |
| Electric vehicles (from Apr 2025) | **No longer exempt** — now pay the standard **£200/year** rate |
| Cars with new list price over £40,000 (petrol/diesel) or £50,000 (EV) | **Expensive Car Supplement:** extra **£440/year** on top of standard rate, for five years |

---

## 3. Insurance (The Group System)

Car insurance is a legal requirement. In the UK, every car model is assigned an **Insurance Group from 1 to 50**.

| Group Range | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Cheap to insure | 1.0L VW Up!, base model Ford Fiesta |
| 40–50 | Extremely expensive to insure | High-performance vehicles, luxury SUVs |

**The Trap:** A 15-year-old BMW might cost only £2,000 to buy. But if it sits in Insurance Group 35, a young driver could be quoted **£3,000/year just to insure it**. Always run a quote before buying.

---

## 4. Depreciation (The Silent Killer)

Depreciation isn't a monthly bill, but it is often the **single biggest cost of motoring**.

- A brand new car loses up to **20% of its value** the second you drive it off the forecourt
- It can lose **50% of its value within the first three years**
- A £30,000 car might be worth £15,000 three years later — that's effectively **£5,000/year** just to own it

**The Strategy:** Buy a used car that is **3 to 4 years old**. The first owner has already absorbed the massive initial depreciation hit, but the car is still modern, safe, and reliable.
