# Lesson 35: Maximising Allowances

You've waited 30 days. Your emotions have settled, and your windfall is sitting safely in a savings account. Now, it's time to **protect it from the taxman**.

If you leave a large sum in a standard savings or investment account, you may have to pay Income Tax on the interest or Capital Gains Tax on your profits. Fortunately, there are two legal "tax wrappers" to shield your money.

---

## 1. The £20,000 ISA Shield

An ISA (Individual Savings Account) is a tax wrapper. Once your cash is inside an ISA, all the interest you earn (Cash ISA) or all investment growth and dividends (Stocks & Shares ISA) are **100% tax-free**.

- **Annual allowance:** £20,000 per tax year
- **Use it or lose it:** The tax year runs April 6th to April 5th. If you don't use your £20k allowance by April 5th, you **lose it forever** — it does not roll over

**The Strategy:** If you inherit £40,000 in March, put £20,000 into an ISA immediately. Then on April 6th (when the new tax year starts and your allowance resets), move the remaining £20,000 in. Your entire windfall is now tax-free for life.

---

## 2. The Pension Boost (Free Government Money)

If you don't need the money until retirement (currently age 55, rising to 57 in 2028), putting your windfall into a pension is arguably the most lucrative move you can make.

When you put money into a **SIPP** (Self-Invested Personal Pension) or workplace pension, the government gives you **Tax Relief** — they refund the income tax you paid on that money and add it to your pension pot.

| Tax Rate | You Pay In | Government Adds | Total in Pot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic rate | £80 | £20 | £100 |
| Higher/Additional rate | £80 | £20 (+ more claimable via tax return) | £100+ |

**The Limits:** You can contribute up to 100% of your annual earnings, capped at a maximum of **£60,000 per tax year** (the Annual Allowance).

---

## 3. Paying Off High-Interest Debt First

Before maxing out your ISA or pension, look at your debts. If you have credit cards or personal loans charging **15% to 40% interest**, paying these off is the ultimate "tax-free return."

No safe savings account or ISA will ever beat the interest you are losing to expensive debt. **Clear the bad debt first, then wrap the rest in your ISA and pension allowances.**
