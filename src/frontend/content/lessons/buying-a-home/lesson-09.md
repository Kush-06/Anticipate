# Lesson 9: The Lifetime ISA (LISA)

**The Mechanics of the Government Bonus**

The Lifetime ISA is a tax-free savings account designed to accelerate your deposit savings. You must be aged **between 18 and 39** to open one.

---

## How It Works

- Maximum deposit: **£4,000 per tax year** (April 6th to April 5th)
- The government adds a **25% bonus** to whatever you save, paid monthly

| Your Contribution | Government Bonus |
|---|---|
| £100/month | £25/month |
| £4,000/year (maximum) | £1,000/year |
| £4,000/year from age 18 to 50 | **£32,000 in free government money** |

---

## Cash LISA vs. Stocks & Shares LISA

You can hold your LISA in cash or invest it in the stock market.

**MoneySavingExpert rule of thumb:** If you plan to buy a house in the **next 5 years**, stick to a Cash LISA so your deposit isn't at the mercy of sudden stock market crashes.

---

## The Three Deal-Breaker Rules

The LISA comes with incredibly strict rules. Breaking them means losing the bonus and some of your own money.

**1. The £450,000 Price Cap**
You can only use a LISA for a property costing **£450,000 or less**. If the property costs £450,001, your LISA becomes invalid for the purchase.

**2. The First-Time Buyer Definition**
You must have **never owned property anywhere in the world**. Even inheriting a 5% share of a grandparent's house legally disqualifies you.

**3. The Brutal 25% Withdrawal Penalty**
If you withdraw your LISA funds for anything other than a qualifying first home (or waiting until age 60), you are hit with a **25% penalty on the total amount**.

> **Why the penalty eats your savings:** Save £4,000, government adds £1,000 — balance is £5,000. Withdraw for a car: 25% of £5,000 = £1,250 taken back. You get £3,750. You have literally lost **£250 of your own money**.
