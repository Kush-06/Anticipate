# Lesson 8: Mortgages 101

**What is a Mortgage and How Much Can You Borrow?**

A mortgage is a long-term loan secured against your property. The standard mortgage term used to be 25 years, but today many first-time buyers opt for 30 or 35 years to make monthly payments more affordable — though this means paying much more interest overall.

---

## The Income Multiplier

When deciding how much you can borrow, lenders use an **Income Multiplier**. Governed by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), most high-street lenders cap maximum borrowing at **4.5× your annual gross salary**.

- **Single applicant:** £30,000 × 4.5 = maximum loan of **£135,000**
- **Joint applicants:** (£30,000 + £35,000) × 4.5 = maximum loan of **£292,500**

> Some specialist lenders may offer 5× or 5.5×, but this is usually reserved for high earners (typically over £60,000/year) or specific professions like doctors and lawyers.

---

## Loan-to-Value (LTV) Explained

Your LTV is the ratio of what you are borrowing compared to the property's total value.

**Example:** Buy a £250,000 house with a £25,000 deposit (10%) — you need a mortgage for £225,000. Your LTV is **90%**.

**The Golden LTV Rule:** Mortgages are priced in 5% tiers (95%, 90%, 85%, etc.). Every time you drop into a lower LTV tier, lenders view you as lower risk and offer a cheaper interest rate. A 95% LTV mortgage is expensive; a 60% LTV unlocks the best rates on the market.

---

## Fixed vs. Tracker vs. Standard Variable Rate (SVR)

| Mortgage Type | How It Works | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed Rate** | Your interest rate and monthly payments are locked and cannot change, regardless of the economy. | Buyers on a tight budget who need absolute certainty about their outgoings. |
| **Tracker Rate** | Your rate tracks the Bank of England's base rate (e.g., Base Rate + 1%). Payments rise and fall with it. | Buyers with financial breathing room who want to benefit if rates drop. |
| **SVR (Standard Variable Rate)** | The lender's default rate. You are moved onto this automatically when your Fixed or Tracker deal expires. | Nobody. SVRs are punishingly expensive. Always remortgage before hitting the SVR. |
